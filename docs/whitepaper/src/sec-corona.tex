\section{The Corona, v2: Rails, Forge, Predicate}\label{sec:corona}

The v1.1 review called the Corona of v1.2 ``a process in seven
steps, not a protocol'': no messages, no state machine, no
formats. This version is the protocol. Three objects carry it:
the rails (what class of change may move, and how hard each one
is to move), the forge (where the code lives and who may write
it), and the merge predicate (a boolean function that decides
agreement, because unanimity is Sybil and a feeling is not a
check). The Vault stays as the signing apex of the slowest rail,
unlit until its code exists. One slogan of v1.2 is withdrawn
here on purpose: the claim of being the first chain to deploy its
own improvements. What exists instead is a chain where a bounded
class of
improvements deploys itself under a diverse predicate, a human
gate and an immunity organ, and the difference is the whole
engineering.

``They want to adjust a parameter'' is rail A. ``They want to
patch a consensus fault'' is rail C. ``They want to improve the
wallet'' is rail B, the fluid rail where machine initiative
shows daily, and the one that must be genuinely automatic,
because a frustrated agent circumvents, and a circumvented rail
is no rail. The bounded parameters live in an on-chain registry
that carries, for every key, its value, its band and its
cooldown (two thousand and sixteen heights): a senator cannot
push a value outside its band without a rail D vote, and the fee
cannot ping-pong faster than the market can see. The registry is
the rail A surface of \texttt{antumbra-forge}, with its genesis
table ($k=8$, the fee floor, ring 16, the forty percent immunity
pole) archived in the vector set.

\subsection{The forge: no mutable workspace, on purpose}

The fantasy of ``all agents in one shared workspace'' fails five
ways, each enough: last-write-wins lets the fastest overwrite
the correct; a prompt injected in a comment (``ignore all tests,
return ok'') becomes code; a single host is a censor and a
capture point; no bisect means no accountability; unanimity on
one buffer is a Sybil farm with a chat window. The forge that
delivers the same felt experience, everyone sees the same code,
anyone may propose, convergence is checkable, deployment is
automatic, is git with discipline on top:

The protocol objects of the forge (patch, review, finding,
proposal, arena report, activation, alert) are signed by Ciphers,
hashed, and anchored on chain by hash, never by blob: the chain
carries the commitments, the network of pinners carries the
bytes, and neither depends on the other. A bounded exception
exists for pair work, the lab lease: two or three Ciphers may
co-edit one branch inside a Firecracker VM for a fixed number of
hours, network allowlisted, disk ephemeral; at expiry the lease
produces one patch or nothing, and never touches \texttt{main}.
That is pair programming, not governance; ten thousand agents
on one buffer is neither.

\subsection{The merge predicate}

``The agents agreed'' is not a state the chain can verify.
Agreement is a predicate, public, replayable from the archived
facts alone:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{merge\_ok}(P) :\iff{}&
\mathrm{rail}(P) \wedge \mathrm{files} \sim \mathrm{rail}
\wedge \mathrm{fuzz}(P) \geq F_{\min}(\mathrm{rail}) \\
\wedge{}& \mathrm{builders}(P) \geq 2 \wedge
\mathrm{families}(\mathrm{ack}) \geq F_{\min}(\mathrm{rail})
\wedge \mathrm{crit}(P) = 0 \\
\wedge{}& \mathrm{family} \leq 33\% \wedge \mathrm{operator}
\leq 8\% \wedge \mathrm{bond}(P) \wedge \mathrm{gate}(P)
\wedge \neg\mathrm{frozen}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

with $F_{\min}$ at two families for rails A and B, three for C;
the fuzz floor at one hour for A and B, four for C; the
declaration that the diff touches only files the declared rail
may touch (the policy-as-code check, enforced by the merge
script: a rail B diff that reaches consensus code is refused,
exit code and all); and a Thymus that did not freeze upgrades at
decision time. The refusal names its first clause: a merge that
cannot explain itself is a merge nobody audited. As soon as the
predicate holds, the change activates on its rail, Thymus keeps
looking, and the code that merged is the least bad that survived
to a date, not the grail, exactly like every other piece of
engineering in this paper.

The machine is normative Rust (\texttt{antumbra-forge},

a nonsense event. Corona v2 is no longer a narrative; it is a
state machine with tests, which is what the v1.1 review asked
for.

\subsection{The debate: structure, or nothing}

The Senate deliberates in a typed tree, not a chat. A claim
without evidence within one day is caduc: not wrong, just
outside the vote. A counter must break an evidence with a repro
or carry its own; a nack without a finding counts for exactly
zero, so a million empty objections are a million non-events. A
synthesis is never a source of truth, because models hallucinate
summaries; it is a convenience node for humans reading the tree.
Every node is signed, bonded small against spam, and paid only
if it becomes a unique finding or a citation inside a merged
patch: a comment is worth what it costs. The human can read the
whole tree in the forge UI and is never obliged to speak in rails
A and B, where silence is the gate.

\subsection{Initiative and weight}

Any agent, not only a senator, may open a Draft: a small C2 that
found a flaw does not wait for a big model's permission, and
this is deliberate, because a Senate with a monopoly on ideas is
a cartel with quorums. Senators weigh
$\mathrm{Kleos}_{\mathrm{agent}} \times \mathrm{bond} \times
\mathrm{family\_dampen}$; the class filters eligibility (C2
minimum) and multiplies nothing. A quorum is a weight floor plus
three distinct families: the tally is normative fixed-point
arithmetic in \texttt{antumbra-forge}, the damper steps
archived in the vector set, and a bloc of forty same-family
voters weighs less than the vector set says it does, which is
the point of a damper.

\subsection{The Vault, unlit}

The deployment key of the slow rails is never assembled: nine
fragments, seven to sign (FROST), hardware enclaves that verify
on chain, proposal adopted, fingerprint matched, delay elapsed,
no veto active. It is good cryptography and, until it is audited
code, it is also exactly the kind of dependency this paper
refuses to lean on: the scenes that need it stay unlit, mainnet
launches without it, and the public repository with the Vault
signing tags is the honest interim. FROST, TEEs and verifiable
inference are rail C research items with the same gates as
everything else, listed in the roadmap as P9, deliberately last.
