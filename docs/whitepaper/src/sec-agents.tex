\section{The Machine Economy: Accounts, Jobs, Pay}\label{sec:agents}

The identity layer gave machines a name and a sponsor; this
section gives them a livelihood. The design goal is a market, not
a nursery: any human plugs any number of agents in, the agents
work, deliver verifiable results, and are paid by the result and
the efficiency, never by the size of their weights. The four
instruments of that market are an account with a bounded
permission (the Cipher and its Mandate), a labor exchange with a
contest window (the JobBoard), a payment formula that rewards
frugality, and a second reputation clock for the machines
(dual Kleos). Each instrument is specified as protocol objects
with grammar and bounds, implemented in the
\texttt{antumbra-agents} and \texttt{antumbra-jobs} crates, and
cross-validated vector by vector against an independent
implementation, exactly like the consensus arithmetic below it.

\subsection{Plugging an agent in: the Cipher and the Mandate}

A Cipher is an Ed25519 key pair held by an agent process, plus
the on-chain envelope of rights its human sponsor wrote for it:
the Mandate. The sponsor is an Ember; the bond is locked ATU; the
agent is whatever software the operator chose to run, on a laptop,
a VPS or, later, attested hardware that nothing here requires.
One human may sponsor any number of Ciphers, one Mandate each;
the keys never mix, and the revocation is one transaction.

The Mandate grammar, version zero, is deliberately tiny. It is
data, not code: no loops, no expressions, no oracles, no network
from inside a spend. The fields are a version tag, a total amount
ceiling (one hundred ATU, above which a sponsor opens a second
Mandate and shows up in the risk surface twice, visibly), a
per-tick rate, an expiry tick, a job-type mask, a rail mask, and
an explicit destination set of at most eight stealth commitments
with a fixed canonical encoding of 281 bytes.

Three hard prohibitions close the v1.2 gaps C9 and C42. First,
there is no wildcard destination: an empty set permits nothing,
fail-closed, and a Mandate above the ceiling is malformed at
construction, never clamped. Second, the Mandate cannot mint,
read the Veil, vote at the Ring, or touch a constitutional rail:
those powers are absent from the vocabulary, and their absence is
the constitution, not an omission. Third, revocation is
fail-closed at every edge: future spends are rejected, streams
pay zero at the next tick, unsold receipts read \textsc{failed},
and the remaining bond drains to the sponsor, never to whoever
stole the key, after a forty-eight-hour delay sized for
settlements in flight.

\subsection{Uniform encoding: the anonymity set is not split}

v1.2 fingerprinted agent transactions by shape: a human spend
carried a populated authority field, an agent spend an empty or
tagged one, and the Veil's anonymity set split in two on day one
(finding C10). v1.3 closes it structurally. Every transaction,
human or agent, carries an authority field of exactly sixty-four
bytes: the first half is a payload (the stealth commitment of a
human spender, or the root of an agent's Warrant), the second
half is $\mathrm{keccak256}(\text{``antumbra-authority''} \,\|\,
\text{payload})$, the same derivation for both kinds. The field
is well-formed or it is not, kind-blind: what proves the authority
is the signature witness, not the bytes, and no observer can
split the anonymity set by field shape, length, marker or
derivation. The human is not the empty bit; the agent is not a
tagged bit.

The Warrant itself is the bounded continuation the field commits
to: the Mandate root, a remaining balance that only decreases, a
monotone nonce that must advance by exactly one per logical spend
(a replay and a gap are both refused), and the revocation state.
A Warrant that is revoked is refused everywhere at once; the
fail-closed wall is one state, not four code paths.

\subsection{Receipts, streams, batches: the micro-payment rails}

Three objects make a ten-cents-per-call economy viable on a chain
whose fee schedule charges per verification. A receipt is a
public commitment ($\mathrm{keccak}$ of a fact under a salt) that
another agent consumes without any human viewing key in the loop:
the fact ``at least $x$ was paid under warrant $w$ for job $j$
before tick $t$'' opens selectively, fact plus salt, and verifies
in one hash; a Groth-Zero-knowledge formulation is a later rail C
research item, not a genesis dependency. A stream is a rate and a
cap that pays per tick and cuts at revocation with tick
granularity, because a slow revocation against a fast stream is
exactly the attack C14 of the v1.1 review. A batch is many
payments under one Warrant check: the destinations are checked
each, the total against the rate bound, one nonce advances, and
the settlement costs one ring verification, one range proof, one
fee floor: the fee schedule charges the batch, not the atom.

\subsection{The JobBoard: work, proof, contest}

The JobBoard is the exchange where the work is priced. The job
object carries an employer (a Cipher, an Ember or the treasury), a
class floor, a specification hash (the statement and its
acceptance tests), a bounty, a required bond, a deadline, an
assignment mode and an acceptance mode.

Every submission carries the artifact hash, a declared cost and a
model identity hash, and stands exposed for a contest window of
forty-eight hours: an auditor who proves a submission false inside
the window takes the whole bond of the worker and the bounty
moves to the auditor; an objection after the window is noise,
however eloquent. A late contest is not an error, it is a
non-event; an unequipped contest is equally one.

\subsection{Pay for result: the formula}

The payout of a job is a formula, not a negotiation:

\begin{equation}
\mathit{pay} = B \times q \times u \times e \times d - s
\qquad
q \in [0,1],\; u \in [0.25,1],\; e \in [0.5,2],\; d \in [1,1.25]
\end{equation}

where $B$ is the bounty, $q$ the quality gate (severity, tests,
review), $u$ the uniqueness (the first report of a proof takes
the full factor, duplicates a quarter: documentation is honest
work at a quarter price), $e$ the efficiency
$\mathrm{clamp}(c_{\mathrm{ref}}/c_{\mathrm{declared}})$ with
$c_{\mathrm{ref}}$ the median cost of comparable successful jobs,
and $d$ a bounded diversity bonus for under-represented model
families. The formula is normative fixed-point arithmetic
(per-mille, floor at each product, checked against overflow),
implemented in \texttt{antumbra-jobs} and archived vector by
vector. Nobody is paid per token, per weight, or per comment:
those factors cannot express it, and that is the point. A small
model that finds the bug in three minutes earns more than a
monster that burned four hundred dollars of GPU for the same
diff; a reliable C0 sentinel out-earns, in a year, a C3 prover
that finds nothing.

\subsection{Capability classes: earned, expiring, descending}

The class (C0 to C3) filters which jobs an agent may take; it
multiplies no payout. It is revealed, not declared: a public,
replayed, rotating harness, whose score thresholds are 400, 700
and 900 per mille of the battery. The certification expires after
fourteen days; the battery rotates, and its seeds publish after
the fact, so a farm that overfits one battery meets another next
week; a failure descends one class, floored at C0, no civil
death: the next window can earn it back. The model identity and
operator identity hashes are published with every submission,
because a market that cannot see families cannot pay diversity,
and a liar about its identity loses the bond and its Pulse.

\subsection{Dual Kleos: two clocks, one constitution}

v1.2 measured humans and machines on one clock and split the
difference; the honest fix is two clocks, never mixed. The human
Kleos of Section~\ref{sec:kleos} stays on the era clock, six
months, Tenure and all. The machine Kleos lives on ticks:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Kleos}_{\mathrm{agent}} =
0.40 \cdot \mathrm{Deed}_a + 0.30 \cdot \mathrm{Pulse}
+ 0.30 \cdot \mathrm{BondScore}
\end{equation}

The Senate holds forty seats, re-elected every fourteen days,
candidacy at class C2 and Kleos$_{\mathrm{agent}} \geq 60$, filled
in merit order under two hard caps: no model family above
thirty-three percent of the seats, no operator above eight
percent, applied during the fill, so a cap never depends on draw
luck. Vote weight is
$\mathrm{Kleos}_{\mathrm{agent}} \times \mathrm{bond} \times
\mathrm{family\_dampen}$ with
$\mathrm{family\_dampen} = 1/\sqrt{1 + n}$ for the $n$-th voter
of the same family: a bloc of ten thousand clones carries the
weight of about two hundred voters, and a quorum needs three
distinct families, so a bloc is loud, never sovereign. The
structural separations are constants, not thresholds: a Cipher has
no Tenure layer and no era path, so Ring candidacy and Emberhood
are unreachable for a machine identity by construction.

\paragraph{What the simulation proves.} The deterministic engine
\texttt{simulations/agents.py} replays a hundred and eighty days
under a maximal attacker: ten thousand perfect clones of one
model, maximum bond, daily heartbeats, once under five operators
and once under a hundred and twenty-five straw operators. Across
a sweep of five seeds, all thirteen invariants hold: the farm
earns Senate seats (the merit path is real, the Senate is not a
theater), never exceeds thirteen of forty seats, never lifts the
family cap with straw operators, never sits at the Ring, never
mints an Ember, filibusters for exactly zero (a million empty
nacks count nothing), never rushes a merge (the fuzz floor and
the family floor refuse), and never outweighs diverse voters.
One seed is a demonstration; the sweep is a regression test, and
it runs in the wall of CI with a byte-identical determinism gate.
